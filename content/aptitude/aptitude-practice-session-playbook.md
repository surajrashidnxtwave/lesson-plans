# Aptitude Practice Session Playbook — Pull-Help Model

**For instructors.** Run this script every practice block, whatever topic's question set is loaded that day. Same ritual every time, so students walk in already knowing how the hour works.

Text in quotes is what you say out loud. This doc is topic-agnostic on purpose — swap the question set, never the ritual.

---

## The one thing that makes this different from a normal practice block

**Students work independently by default. You do not circulate. You do not go looking for stuck students.**

You help only when a student brings a problem to you. Until that happens, you stay visibly available at a fixed spot — but you do not walk the room, you do not hover, and you do not open with "any doubts?"

This is deliberate. A student stuck for a few minutes, thinking, re-reading, trying a second approach — that is the session working, not a problem to rescue them from. If you go hunting for stuck students, you're telling the room the struggle is something to be avoided. It isn't. Sitting with a hard problem before asking for help is the actual skill this format is training.

**The help signal is a raised hand.** Not a red slip — a slip only works if someone's walking past to spot it, and nobody's walking past anymore. A raised hand is visible from your fixed spot the instant it goes up, and it self-orders the queue: first hand up, first served.

---

## Before students arrive (5 min)

1. **Board** — draw the check-history table (see *The check*, below). That's the only thing that goes on the board before the session starts.
2. **Timer** — visible countdown, projected or on the board.
3. **Answer key** — ready, but not visible. It surfaces only at the exit gate.
4. **Your spot** — front of the room, or a fixed desk. Pick one and stay there. You move only when a student calls you up to it — never the other way round.

> **Students already know where the question set lives.** They open the learning portal on their own laptops and navigate to today's practice from the course contents — nothing to project, nothing to hand out. Your job at launch is to confirm everyone is actually *on* it, not to tell them where it is.

---

## Never do these

- ❌ Circulate. Walk the aisles, glance at screens, hover near anyone.
- ❌ Ask "any doubts?" — it invites a false no and breaks the pull model's whole logic.
- ❌ Answer a raised hand by solving the problem for them. Ask, don't tell.
- ❌ Skip a check because the room looks busy — busy is not the same as on-pace.
- ❌ Let the queue turn into a scrum. One student at your spot at a time; everyone else waits their turn, working.
- ❌ Call out who's stuck, or who hasn't raised a hand yet. The count is the pressure — naming a person kills the room.

---

## The help queue

**Order:** strictly first-hand-up, first-served. If two hands go up close together and it's genuinely unclear, ask the room — "who was first?" — and move on. Don't relitigate it.

**When a hand goes up:** acknowledge it immediately, even if you're mid-help with someone else — a nod, a raised finger, "I see you, one second." That's enough to stop a second hand from turning into a wave of hands. Then finish who you're with.

**At your spot, 2 minutes max per student.** Two minutes is enough for a nudge, not a solution. What you say:

| What they show you | Say exactly this |
|---|---|
| Doesn't know where to start | *"Read me the problem in your own words."* |
| Has a partial attempt | *"Show me where you got stuck. What was the last thing you calculated?"* |
| Has an answer, unsure if it's right | *"Walk me through how you got there."* |
| Wants you to just check the final number | *"Not yet. Show me the working first."* |
| Made an arithmetic slip | Point at the line: *"Check that step again."* — don't correct it for them |

**Never solve the problem on their sheet.** Point at the step, ask the question that exposes the gap, then:

> *"Go back and try that. If it's still not landing in a couple of minutes, raise your hand again — you're not losing your place in line by coming back."*

That last line matters — say it every time. It tells them a second ask isn't a failure.

**If the queue backs up** (three or more hands at once): don't panic-speed through students. Say once, to the room:

> *"I've got three of you waiting. I'll get to each of you — keep working on something else while you wait, don't just sit."*

Then work the queue at the same 2-minute pace. A backed-up queue for a minute or two is normal, not a sign you're behind.

## Session ritual (flexible length)

Built as fractions of total session time, so it stretches or compresses cleanly. Worked example below assumes 60 minutes — for a 90-minute block, multiply every mark by 1.5; for 45 minutes, by 0.75.

| Fraction of session | 60-min example | Block |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8% | 0–5 | **Launch** |
| 8–25% | 5–15 | Work stretch 1 |
| 25% | 15 | **Check 1** |
| 25–50% | 15–30 | Work stretch 2 |
| 50% | 30 | **Check 2** |
| 50–75% | 30–45 | Work stretch 3 |
| 75% | 45 | **Check 3** |
| 75–92% | 45–55 | Work stretch 4 / live debrief |
| 92–100% | 55–60 | **Exit gate** |

Run a check at roughly every quarter of the session — three checks minimum for anything 40 minutes or longer. Shorter than that, run two.

---

### Launch

Stand at your fixed spot. Don't start until the room is quiet.

> *"Laptops open, learning portal, today's question set. Open it now, all of you. I'll give you thirty seconds."*

Wait the thirty seconds. Scan the room, say what you see:

> *"Two of you still aren't on it. I'll wait."*

Then state the deal, all in one go:

> *"Here's how this works. You solve on your own. If you're stuck, you decide when to ask — there's no rule that says you have to wait, and no rule that says you have to rush. Raise a hand, I'll come to you at the front — you walk up, we look at it together for two minutes, then you go back and try again. Every [fifteen] minutes I'll stop the room and ask how many problems you've finished — hands, not names. Before you leave, you show me a solved problem with the working shown, checked against the answer key. That's the hour."*

Countdown to start:

> *"Three, two, one — go."*

---

### Work stretch

Silent, solo work. Timer visible. You're at your spot, watching for hands, not walking.

> *"Solo and silent from here. Timer's on the screen."*

You have three jobs during a stretch, and only these three:

1. Watch for raised hands.
2. Run the help queue when hands go up (see above).
3. Notice, without commenting on it, which problem keeps coming up at your spot — you need that for the live debrief.

---

## The check

**Run this at every fraction mark above. It takes 60–90 seconds and it's the thing that keeps the room's pace visible without you ever leaving your spot.**

Use a ladder — one pass gives you the whole distribution:

> *"Everyone stop. Hands up if you've finished at least one."*
> — count out loud —
> *"Keep them up if you've done two."*
> — count —
> *"Three."*
> — count —

Write the ladder into the check-history table on the board:

```
CHECK 1 (25%)  ·  1+: 38   2+: 19   3+: 6
CHECK 2 (50%)  ·  1+: 44   2+: 31   3+: 14
CHECK 3 (75%)  ·  1+: 47   2+: 39   3+: 25
```

Then two things, every time:

- **Compare to the last check, out loud.** *"Two-or-more went from nineteen to thirty-one. That's the room working."*
- **Name the sticking point you saw at the help spot** — not from circulating, from who came up to you. *"A few of you who came up were stuck on the same step in problem four. You're not alone — hold it, we'll look at it before we finish."*

**Rules**
- Never name a student who hasn't started. The count is the pressure, not the person.
- Never skip a check because the queue is busy — pause the queue for the 60–90 seconds, run the check, resume.
- If a number goes flat between checks, that's a signal to change something at the next stretch (harder problem attracting everyone, a shared misconception) — not a reason to say the room is disappointing.

---

## Live debrief (before the exit gate)

Solve the problem that actually came up most at your help spot — not one you picked before the session.

- Work it from scratch, on the board, talking through your thinking out loud.
- Make a real mistake if one happens naturally, and read it back: *"Wait — that's wrong, here's why."* Don't fake it, but don't hide a real one either.
- Pull the next step from the room instead of supplying it: *"What's the next number I need?"*

> *"If you already attempted this one, this is confirming it for you. If you didn't get to it, this is the version you'll have to redo alone tonight — and it'll take three times as long without today's version in your head first."*

---

## What "done" looks like — the exit gate

> *"Before you leave: one problem, fully worked, final answer boxed. Check it against the key on the screen. If it matches, you're done. If it doesn't, that's your one question for tomorrow's session."*

Reveal the answer key now, not before. Students self-check.

Stand near the door — visible, not blocking anyone.

> *"Show me the sheet on your way out — worked steps and a boxed answer, that's all I'm checking for. I'm not re-solving it, just confirming it's there."*

A glance is enough — you're confirming a boxed answer with visible working exists, not re-grading correctness at the door. Anyone whose self-check against the key didn't match gets sent off with:

> *"That one's your first question tomorrow, not tonight's homework problem. Bring it back."*

Then, before they're gone:

> *"Last thing — which problem are you starting with next time? Say it to yourself, or write it at the top of your sheet."*

Read the board on the way out:

> *"Two-or-more went from nineteen to thirty-one to thirty-nine today. Same time next session."*

## One-page strip

| Fraction | Block | You are doing |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8% | **Launch** | Fixed spot, state the deal, scan screens, countdown to start |
| 8–25% | **Work stretch 1** | Watching for hands only — no circulating |
| 25% | **Check 1** | Ladder count → board, compare, name sticking point from the queue |
| 25–50% | **Work stretch 2** | Same — hands, queue, notice patterns |
| 50% | **Check 2** | Ladder count, compare to Check 1 out loud |
| 50–75% | **Work stretch 3** | Same |
| 75% | **Check 3** | Ladder count, read the full trend off the board |
| 75–92% | **Live debrief** | Solve the queue's actual sticking point, live, on the board |
| 92–100% | **Exit gate** | Reveal answer key, self-check, glance-confirm one boxed answer, next-session commitment |

**Help is pull, always.** You never leave your spot to find a student. A raised hand brings them to you — 2 minutes, a nudge, back to their seat. Three checks minimum, every block, no exceptions.
