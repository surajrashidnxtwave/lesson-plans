# Practice Session Playbook — 60 Minutes

**For instructors.** Run this script every practice block. Same ritual every time.

Text in quotes is what you say out loud.

---

## Before students arrive (5 min)

1. **Board** — draw the progress tracker.
2. **Timer** — visible countdown on the projector.
3. **Red slips** — a stack of red paper slips at the end of each row.

> **Students already know where the practice lives.** They open the learning portal on their own laptops and navigate to today's practice from the course contents — you don't project a URL and they don't need one. Your job at launch is to make sure everyone is actually *on* it, not to tell them where it is.

**Progress board:** one column per pod, one row per problem. Anchors add a tick when a member finishes. No names — counts only.

```
POD    A    B    C    D    E    F    G    H
P1     |||| |||  |||| |||| ||   |||| |||  ||||
P2     ||   |    |||  ||   |    |||  ||   |||
P3          |    |         |    |         |
MCQ    |||| |||  |||  |||| ||   |||| |||  ||||
```

---

## Pods (set up once, keep all term)

**Groups of 7–8 students. A pod is a block of seats, not a group students choose.** If you sit there, you're in that pod. Nobody picks, nobody is left out, nobody moves.

Number of pods ≈ class size ÷ 7.

| Class size | Pods | Per pod |
|---|---|---|
| 50 | 7 | 7–8 |
| 70 | 9 | 7–8 |
| 85 | 11 | 7–8 |
| 100 | 13 | 7–8 |

### 5-minute setup, once for the term

1. **Map the room before the block.** Sketch the seating, draw pod boundaries — usually 2 rows × 4 seats, or one row split in half. Equal blocks.
2. **Label physically.** Tape a sheet with the pod letter at the aisle end of each block. Leave it up all term.
3. **Announce it, 30 seconds:** *"Look at the letter nearest you. That's your pod for the rest of this course. You don't move, you don't swap. If you're sitting here, you're in that pod."*
4. **Anchors** — ask each pod for a volunteer. If nobody volunteers, **the student in the aisle seat is the anchor** — closest to the board, which is the job's only physical requirement. Rotate weekly by moving one seat along.
5. **Match the board to the room.** Board columns run left-to-right in the same order as the pods. Pod A is the far-left block *and* the far-left column.

### When things shift

- **Someone's absent** — pod is smaller today. Don't re-pod, don't merge.
- **Anchor's absent** — aisle seat takes over. That's why the rule is positional, not a named person.
- **Someone sits in the wrong block** — they're in that pod today. Not worth correcting.
- **New joiner** — they sit somewhere, that's their pod. No admin.

### The anchor's job — 3 things, none of them teaching

1. Mark ticks on the board when a pod member finishes.
2. Escalate a red slip to you when the pod can't resolve something in 2 minutes.
3. At the exit gate, confirm each pod member has one passing output.

Tell anchors once: *"You are not a tutor. You are not expected to know the answers. You count, and you escalate. That's it."*

---

## Never do these

- ❌ Stand silently at the front.
- ❌ Ask *"Any doubts?"*
- ❌ Walk the aisles without stopping.
- ❌ Take a student's keyboard and fix it yourself.
- ❌ Let the block start by itself.
- ❌ Solve a problem on the board before students attempt it.

# The 60-minute script

## 0–5 · LAUNCH

Stand at the front. Do not start until the room is quiet.

> *"Laptops open, learning portal, today's practice. Right now, all of you. I'm going to look at the room in thirty seconds and I want to see that page on every screen."*

Wait the thirty seconds. Scan the room. Say what you see:

> *"Row three, four of you are still on something else. I'll wait."*

Then state the deal:

> *"Here's the hour. In eight minutes every single person here will have finished problem one — I'm going to walk you into it. Then you work. At forty-eight minutes I solve the problem that most of you got stuck on, and if you haven't attempted it by then, watching me solve it will do nothing for you. Before you leave, you show me or your pod anchor one problem that runs. That's the deal."*

---

## 5–12 · PROBLEM 1 TOGETHER

Nobody works alone yet.

1. **Read problem 1 aloud.** The whole statement.
2. **Ask for the approach, not the code:** *"Don't give me syntax. In plain English, what does this program have to do?"* Take two answers.
3. **Everyone types it themselves.** Say the structure out loud. Do **not** type it on the projector. *"Type it. I'm not showing you — you have it."*
4. **Countdown:** *"Three, two, one — everyone hit run."*
5. **Hands up who got the right output.** Count out loud. *"That's fifty-one of you who just wrote a working program. Anchors, mark P1."*

**If a student's doesn't run** — do not fix it. Say *"read the error message and tell me the line number."*

---

## 12–15 · SET THE NORM + CHECK 1

> *"Next fifteen minutes: solo and silent. Timer's on the screen. If you're stuck for more than two minutes — two, not ten — put a red slip on your screen. Don't raise your hand, don't turn around. Red slip. I or your anchor will come to you."*

> *"And every fifteen minutes I'm going to stop you and ask how many you've done. Hands, not names. Get used to it."*

Then run **Check 1** on problem 1 — see *The 15-minute check* below.

---

# The 15-minute check

**Run this at 15, 30 and 45 minutes. Every block, same way.** It takes 90 seconds and it is the main thing separating this from a silent room.

Use a **ladder** — one pass gives you the whole distribution:

> *"Everyone stop. Hands up if you've finished at least one."*
> — count —
> *"Keep them up if you've done two."*
> — count —
> *"Three."*
> — count —
> *"Four."*

Say each number out loud as you count it, and write the ladder in the corner of the board:

```
15 min   ·  1+: 71   2+: 44   3+: 12
30 min   ·  1+: 78   2+: 61   3+: 38   4+: 15
45 min   ·  1+: 80   2+: 72   3+: 55   4+: 31
```

Then do two things:

- **Compare to the last check, out loud.** *"Two-or-more went from forty-four to sixty-one in fifteen minutes. Keep going."*
- **Name the sticking point you saw while circulating.** *"About a third of you are stuck on the same thing in problem three. You're not alone — hold it, we solve it at forty-eight."*

**Rules**
- **Never name a student who hasn't started.** The count is the pressure. Naming costs you the room.
- **Never skip a check because the room looks busy.** The check is what makes the room look busy.
- If a number goes flat between checks, that's your signal to change something — not to say it's disappointing.

---

## 15–30 · WORK STRETCH 1 · Route A

Silent solo work. Timer visible. You circulate.

**Route A** = the half of the room you normally reach last. Back and far corner first.

**What to say at each desk:**

| What you see | Say exactly this |
|---|---|
| Problem open, no code typed | *"Read me the first line you're going to write."* |
| Code typed, not run | *"Run it. Let's see what it says."* |
| Error on screen | *"Read the error out loud. What line?"* |
| Looks finished | *"Show me your output."* |
| Not on the platform at all | *"Which problem are you on?"* — flat, no scolding |

**Rules for yourself:**
- Never touch their keyboard. Point at the line, ask the question, let them type.
- 90 seconds max per student.
- **Note which problem people are stuck on.** You need it at 48 min.

---

## 30–33 · CHECK 2

Run the ladder. Compare to Check 1 out loud. Name the sticking point.

> *"Anchors, update your columns."*

---

## 33–45 · WORK STRETCH 2 · Route B

> *"Next twelve minutes you may talk to your pod. Quietly. Explaining it to someone else is how you find out whether you actually understand it."*

**Route B** = the half you didn't cover. Same scripted questions.

**Priority:** red slips first, then anyone whose screen hasn't changed since Route A.

**For a student stuck and frustrated** — hand them to a peer:
> *"Priya finished this one two minutes ago. Priya, walk him through your approach — don't give him the code."*

---

## 45–48 · CHECK 3

Run the ladder one last time. Read the full trend off the board — 15 → 30 → 45.

> *"Two-or-more: forty-four, then sixty-one, now seventy-two. That's the hour working."*

This is the number you'll open the next block with.

---

## 48–55 · LIVE SOLVE

Solve the problem you actually saw people stuck on — not one you picked in advance.

- Type it live, from scratch, talking through your thinking.
- **Make a deliberate mistake and read the error message aloud.**
- Take the next step from the room: *"What goes on line two?"*

> *"If you attempted this before now, this is landing for you. If you didn't, this is the part you'll have to redo at home — and it'll take you three times as long alone."*

---

## 55–60 · EXIT GATE

> *"Before you leave: one problem that runs, shown to me or your pod anchor. Anchors, you're confirming your six."*

Stand at the door. Don't block anyone — just be visible.

Then:

> *"Last thing. In the chat — or out loud to your pod — say which problem you're doing first next session."*

Read the board on the way out:

> *"Board says fifty-two of you finished two or more today. Started at eleven three weeks ago. Same time tomorrow."*

## One-page strip

| Time | Block | You are doing |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Launch** | Front of room, scripted start, scan screens, state the deal |
| 5–12 | **Problem 1 together** | Read aloud, take approach from room, countdown to run, count hands |
| 12–15 | **Set the norm + CHECK 1** | Solo work, timer, 2-min red-slip rule → **ladder count** |
| 15–30 | **Work stretch 1** | Route A — back and corners first, scripted questions, 90 s cap |
| 30–33 | **CHECK 2** | **Ladder count**, compare to Check 1 out loud, name the sticking point |
| 33–45 | **Work stretch 2** | Route B — pod talk allowed, red slips first, peer handoffs |
| 45–48 | **CHECK 3** | **Ladder count**, read the 15 → 30 → 45 trend off the board |
| 48–55 | **Live solve** | Type live, deliberate mistake, take next steps from the room |
| 55–60 | **Exit gate** | At the door, one passing output each, next-session commitment, read the board |

**Three checks, every block, no exceptions.** 15 · 30 · 45.
